LEARNING CONTRACT

A visitor should be able to trace a token through embeddings, a decoder layer,
final normalization, output scores, and another generation step. They should
understand the separate roles of contextual attention and per-token expert
routing, the residual bypasses, and reuse of cached keys and values.

Use the README's Mixtral-specific explanations and illustrative-data labels.
Make all 32 layers selectable, expand one layer at a time, and preserve layer
identity. Show four query heads per key/value group and two selected experts.
Distinguish learned weights from runtime activations and cache entries.

SPATIAL CONTRACT

Follow the spatial deliverable contract in docs/project-plan.md. Use actual depth
for the 32-layer stack and for inspecting grouped heads and parallel experts.
Thin matrix sheets are appropriate; their grouping, axes, and connections must
explain the computation. Do not confuse layer count with heads, tokens, or experts.

Provide an oblique overview, adjustable stack spacing, layer extraction with a
persistent stack locator, and a face-on matrix reading view. Keep layer/group/token
selection synchronized across the 3D scene and readable HTML details. Prove the
round trip from overview through one attention group and its matrix back to the
same layer and overview. Preserve these states on narrow screens and with reduced
motion. A perspective-styled flat diagram or prerecorded camera tour is not the
spatial deliverable. Validate the untextured spatial slice before adding polish.

Each camera stop needs one main learning objective, visible evidence, essential
labels, a misconception to avoid, and a checkable acceptance criterion. Reveal
mathematical detail on demand. Use an original wireframe or permissioned visual
reference to establish layout before adding materials and effects.
